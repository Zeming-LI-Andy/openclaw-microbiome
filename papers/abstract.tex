\begin{abstract}
Microbiome data analysis requires custom analytical workflows dictated by the
specific biological question, sequencing assay, and intermediate results.
However, executing these workflows requires integrating disparate computational
environments, reference databases, and multi-step bioinformatics software.
Here, we present MicroBioAgent, an autonomous domain-specific agent that
translates biological questions into study-specific pipelines across 16S
amplicon, profiling-based shotgun, and assembly-based metagenomics.
MicroBioAgent dynamically selects and executes bioinformatics tools within
user-controlled environments, iteratively evaluating intermediate outputs to
adapt subsequent analysis steps.
We evaluated MicroBioAgent across three benchmark datasets: (1)~amplicon and
shotgun profiling of tomato rhizosphere microbiomes; (2)~taxonomic and
functional profiling with machine-learning classification in a colorectal
cancer cohort; and (3)~de novo metagenomic assembly, binning of
metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), and metabolic pathway annotation from a
polyethylene degradation study.  Across all benchmarks, MicroBioAgent
successfully reproduced the primary biological conclusions of the original
studies, with minor variations in taxon detection and statistical metrics
driven by differences in tool selection and database versions.
In robustness evaluations across 27 independent sessions spanning three large
language models and three prompt complexity levels, MicroBioAgent successfully
completed 98.8\% of analytical tasks with highly consistent diversity and
differential-abundance estimates.  Furthermore, benchmarking our background
execution mechanism demonstrated that asynchronous task management reduced
token consumption by over 50\%, while supporting mid-run plan adjustments and
automated continuation upon task completion.  Overall, MicroBioAgent
establishes a flexible, adaptive framework for end-to-end microbiome data
analysis without relying on rigid pipeline templates.
\end{abstract}
